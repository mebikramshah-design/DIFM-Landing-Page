Project Overview

An integrated FM mandate across six distinct operating environments.

Milaha Real Estate's portfolio combines a corporate headquarters and showrooms, a high-rise office tower, temperature-controlled warehousing and residential communities. Each setting places different demands on building services: workplace comfort, cold-storage continuity, safe shared amenities and timely support for occupiers.

This profile sets out Darwish Interserve Facility Management's integrated FM delivery framework against Milaha's supplied scope of services. The documented portfolio includes Milaha Headquarters, Milaha Logistic City, QN Tower, Villa Complex, Park View Residence and Al Mansoura Apartments. Hard services, cleaning, pest control, landscaping and helpdesk management sit within a performance-based contract framework.

Client and Engagement

Milaha Real Estate is the client-side stakeholder. Its activities include sales, leasing, property management and project development. Scope of Services

How We Deliver

Site teams, specialist contractors and the client — on one work-order system.

The delivery model links site teams, specialist contractors and the client through one work-order system. Staffing must follow operational demand and agreed performance obligations across the six properties.

1

Mobilisation before takeover

The scope calls for mobilisation at least 60 days before handover. The transition includes coordination with the outgoing contractor, condition surveys, review of maintenance records, system familiarisation, CAFM training and preparation of operating procedures. Comprehensive asset capture and population of existing records are required within 20 days from the start of mobilisation.

2

Site teams matched to property risk

Facility management, MEP supervision, technicians, cleaning teams and specialist support are allocated according to each site's requirements. The source includes indicative existing manpower schedules, but explicitly requires the contractor to develop its own deployment plan. Variation and out-of-scope works require a separate team rather than diversion of the dedicated maintenance workforce.

3

Working inside the client's platform

A permanent onsite facility coordinator is required 24/7. Requests, planned work, corrective tasks and variations are raised and updated in Milaha's EPMS. Field staff use handheld devices to attach supporting reports and dated photographs. Daily, weekly and monthly reporting data must come from the CAFM system.

4

Specialist interfaces and asset control

The contractor coordinates OEM and specialist work for life safety, vertical transport, electrical systems, refrigeration and other specialist equipment. Asset records are updated as equipment changes, with a quarterly register submission highlighting additions. Response and resolution times follow the agreed KPI and SLA matrix.

Performance Evidence

The contractual controls and reporting deadlines.

The attachment defines how performance must be demonstrated. It contains no completed work-order volumes, achieved KPI scores, incident closure rates or verified savings. The evidence below therefore records contractual controls and reporting deadlines, rather than delivered outcomes.

1

Planned maintenance supported by asset records

The maintenance programme must connect assets, scheduled tasks, equipment checklists and completed service records. Monthly reporting includes planned versus actual maintenance, corrective work summaries and updated asset information. This gives the client a basis to assess completion and backlog once operating data is available.

3

Safety and specialist evidence

Monthly appendices include fire and life-safety reports in QCDD format, water testing, air-quality and duct-cleaning records, third-party certification, lift maintenance and specialist service reports. The requirement creates an auditable record of inspections and work; it does not by itself demonstrate compliance or certification achieved.

4

Client visibility of service quality

The monthly report must include work-order completion rates, response and resolution times, HSE statistics, customer survey feedback, utilities readings and areas for improvement. Milaha evaluates performance using CAFM records and may apply deductions for non-compliance under the agreed contract framework.

5

Resource and sustainability reporting

Energy and water readings, waste management information and conservation initiatives form part of the reporting requirements. No baseline, consumption reduction or carbon saving is documented, so no environmental outcome is claimed.

Managing Complexity

Challenge and response pairs.

The portfolio creates identifiable operating risks. The following challenge-and-response examples pair documented site characteristics with the controls required by the scope; they are not accounts of incidents already experienced by DIFM.

Cold storage continuity

Challenge

Milaha Logistic City includes 35,000 m² of temperature-controlled warehousing, with conditions specified from −24 °C to +22 °C. Refrigeration faults can affect storage conditions.

Response

The scope requires continuous refrigeration operation, planned and corrective maintenance, specialist coordination and refrigeration-plant parameter records.

Different service environments

Challenge

Office floors, showrooms, logistics operations and residential amenities create different maintenance and access requirements.

Response

Property-specific deployment and maintenance planning align resources with local demand while EPMS provides a common reporting record.

Asset information at takeover

Challenge

The source states that the reference asset list is not guaranteed to be accurate. Missing assets can leave gaps in planned maintenance.

Response

Comprehensive capture within 20 days of mobilisation, electronic labelling and quarterly register submissions establish the required control process.

Specialist systems and occupied properties

Challenge

Life-safety systems, lifts, electrical equipment and residential amenities require coordinated specialist work alongside daily use.

Response

The scope requires specialist support, documented inspections, HSE procedures and maintenance evidence linked to the relevant work order.
